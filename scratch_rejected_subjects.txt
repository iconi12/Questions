LATIN-SCRIPT ANSWER PRECEDENT: row 218 (AMC) is the section's first non-Arabic-script
answer — intentional, noted in its own row. Rule: when an equally-correct Arabic rendering
exists for a fact (e.g. a Gregorian year, which could be written in Hijri instead),
consistency with the rest of the column wins — use the form everything else uses. When no
accepted Arabic form exists for the actual answer (a brand name nobody transliterates,
like AMC), the script is forced by the subject, not a stylistic choice — keep it as-is
and flag it in the note so lead/reviewer don't "correct" it later.

"FIRST X" QUESTION-SHAPE OVERUSE — flagged once the count hit 34/~223 rows (~15% of
the section, 18 of 62 approved 500s at the time, i.e. ~a third of the 500 tier). Not a
rule violation (rule 6 is about subject spread, not question shape) but a real variety
cost: a tier that opens "من هو أول..." / "ما اسم أول..." a third of the time plays as
monotonous even though every fact is distinct. Root cause: the pre-1970 institutional
lane (which never loses rows at Pass 2) most naturally produces "first institution of
type X" questions, so leaning on that lane for 500s quietly concentrated the shape.
Going forward, stay in the pre-1970 institutional lane for sourcing reliability but
VARY THE SHAPE. Alternatives already proven in the file, with examples to imitate:
- what something was called BEFORE a rename — row 101 كاسوك (Aramco's name pre-1944)
- what a thing was renamed TO — row 129 ميناء الملك عبدالعزيز (Dammam port after 1960)
- who COMMANDED an action (not "who was first") — row 120
- who was the LAST of something (آخر, not أول) — row 121 محمد بن طلال, the only "آخر"
  row in the section so far; a nice inversion worth repeating
- what agreement/treaty SETTLED a specific dispute — rows 52, 176
- what a specification/figure WAS (weight, measurement) — row 104
- which body PRECEDED which (succession, not origin) — row 124
- what year something ENDED — row 57 (the neutral zone's legal end)
Before drafting a new 500 in this lane, check whether "أول"/"من هو أول" is about to be
the opening words again — if so, try recasting as one of the above shapes first.

RENAME-SHAPE REFINEMENT (from row 225): the "what was it called before/after" pattern
works cleanest when the old and new names share NO words — row 101 is the model
(أرامكو السعودية in the question, كاسوك as the answer, zero overlap). It's thin when the
old name contains the new one as a substring, because the shared base name then sits in
the question and hands over most of the answer's words for free — row 225 (وزارة الدفاع
→ وزارة الدفاع والطيران) only survives because الطيران is still the one truly untested
element and everything else is forced by the subject, not by choice. Also: this pattern
interacts badly with the 4-word answer cap — a renamed body's FULL official name can
easily run 5 words (وزارة الدفاع والطيران والمفتشية العامة did), forcing a shortened
answer that may be meaningfully easier than the full name, pulling the tier down a notch
(500→300 happened on row 225 for exactly this reason). Check word count AND difficulty
together before committing to which form of a multi-part official name is the answer.

SELF-DESCRIBING-NAME TRAP — now the most common single defect (7 rows killed by it:
وزارة الداخلية, الخطوط السعودية, STC, SEC, GASTAT, بطاقة الهوية الوطنية, الرئاسة العامة
لتعليم البنات in its first draft). Pre-empt it at draft time: before writing any row
whose answer is an organisation, document or authority, write the answer down and ask
whether the question's description is just that name paraphrased. If yes, either (a)
move the blank to a date/figure instead of the name (works when the fact is otherwise
solid — see row 214's fix), (b) attribute a claim instead of asserting it (row 199's
"وفق موسوعة غينيس" pattern), or (c) pick a different entity whose name isn't its function
— سابك, إثراء, نزاهة, الدفاع المدني, الهلال الأحمر all cleared for exactly that reason.
