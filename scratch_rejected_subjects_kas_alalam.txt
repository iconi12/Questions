100-tier batch #2-3 lessons (2026-10-07, reviewer review of 15 more drafted rows):
- THE CORE TEST for a real 100 (reviewer's four questions, apply before drafting):
  1. Would someone who never watches football still know it from TV/news?
  2. Is the answer a mega-name, a country, or a Saudi moment (not an award name,
     ball name, mascot name, or company name — those are all fan-level and
     consistently tested 300 this round: Panini, Eusébio, Jabulani, Klose,
     Ronaldo's 5-WC record, UAE's 1990 fact all got bumped to 300; Gazza got
     bumped to 500 since a British player's nickname means nothing to a Saudi
     audience).
  3. Does the question work without needing a year or number recalled?
  4. Is there only one natural guess (a record invites equally-famous rivals
     as guesses — Ronaldo's "first to score in 5 WCs" invited Messi as a
     guess once Messi later matched the feat, which is part of why it tested
     300 not 100 despite the 2022 anchor keeping the fact itself correct).
- "Captain who lifted the trophy" template: CLOSED, exactly at Maradona
  (100), Casillas (300), Cafu (300), Varela (500). Confirmed by dropping
  Matthäus, Lahm, Bobby Moore, and Cannavaro — all real captains, all fail
  the blurt test (no non-fan can name England's 1966 or Italy's 2006
  captain). Maradona alone passes because HE personally is the household
  name, not because "captain" is an easy category. Don't draft another
  captain-lifts-trophy row for any other edition.
- OPENING/CLOSING CEREMONY PERFORMERS: tried and failed (Jungkook, Morgan
  Freeman both dropped — Freeman also had a P3 leak from naming Jungkook
  directly in the question text, not just the note). The whole lane fails
  the blurt test the same way the captain template did: "a K-pop member" or
  "an American actor at a ceremony" requires having WATCHED that specific
  ceremony — unlike Waka Waka, which passed because the SONG itself is
  universally known independent of having watched anything. Self-dropped
  Will Smith (2018 closing) too, before reviewer even reached it, applying
  the same reasoning preemptively — "which actor performed at ceremony X"
  is viewing-dependent regardless of how famous the actor is in general.
  Don't draft more ceremony-performer rows; the hook needs to be the SONG
  (if it's genuinely iconic on its own) or the moment, not "who performed."
  CLOSED for every edition checked so far (2018, 2022, 2026) — the "who
  performed/sang at the ceremony" shape itself is the problem, not any one
  edition's lineup, so don't re-try it for 2010/2014 either.
  2022 is also the most overloaded edition (~55 mentions) — new 2022 rows
  need to be exceptional on their own merits even before the ceremony issue.
- THE "BLURT TEST" for pattern (b) (2026-10-07): before drafting, say the
  question out loud and ask what a non-fan would blurt out first. If that
  blurt is the correct answer, it's a 100. If the blurt is a DIFFERENT
  famous name (the tempting-wrong-answer shape), it's 300+ even if the
  actual answer is also somewhat famous. This killed Milla (a non-fan asked
  "oldest player ever to score at a WC" blurts "Ronaldo" — famous, played at
  41 in 2026 — and is wrong). It ALSO killed the captain-template extensions
  beyond Maradona: Matthäus, Lahm, Bobby Moore, and Cannavaro are all real,
  correct captains, but none is a household name in Saudi Arabia, so a
  non-fan has NO name to blurt at all for "who captained Germany/England/
  Italy in year X" — that's not even a tempting-wrong-answer case, just a
  blank. The pattern (b) template ("captain who lifted year X's trophy")
  works ONLY for Maradona, and is otherwise CLOSED — already has Maradona
  (100), Casillas (300), Cafu (300), Varela (500); don't add more editions
  to it. The lesson generalizes: a question SHAPE that worked for one
  legendary name does not transfer its fame to other, less iconic answers
  in the same shape. Test each specific name's Saudi-household-name status
  individually, every time — never copy a working shape onto a new person
  without re-testing.
- CHECKLIST ADDITION (2026-10-07): for ANY "record holder" row, search
  "<record> 2026" specifically before drafting, not just the historical
  record. Pelé's 1970 assist record (6) was broken by Michael Olise (7) at
  2026 — dropped because the question's own framing ("صاحب الرقم القياسي")
  makes the CURRENT holder the only correct answer, and a "حتى نسخة 2022م"
  anchor can't rescue it when the underlying stat itself is soft for the
  older era anyway (assist counts are only reliably recorded from 1966 on).
  Same root cause as the Klose case. This is now a standing pre-draft step,
  not optional.
